How many steps a day do you really need?

Updated 20 November 2026 · 6 min read

Short answer: for most adults, around 7,000 steps a day captures most of the health benefit. A large 2025 review linked 7,000 steps a day to a 47% lower risk of death from any cause compared with 2,000. Benefits keep rising a little beyond that, so 10,000 is still a fine goal, but it isn't the magic number. If you're well under 7,000 now, every extra 1,000 counts.

Where 10,000 steps came from

The famous number didn't start in a lab. In 1965 a Japanese company sold a pedometer called the manpo-kei, which means "10,000-step meter", with the slogan "Let's walk 10,000 steps a day!" It was a round, memorable number, and it stuck. At the time, there were no studies behind it.

The good news is that researchers have since tested it with real data, and walking turns out to be even more worthwhile than the slogan suggests, at lower numbers than you might think.

What the research says

Two large analyses, both published in The Lancet Public Health, give the clearest picture:

  • 2025, 57 studies (Ding et al.): compared with 2,000 steps a day, 7,000 steps a day was associated with a 47% lower risk of death from any cause, along with lower risk of heart disease, dementia, depressive symptoms, type 2 diabetes and falls. For most outcomes the benefit grew quickly up to about 5,000–7,000 steps and then levelled off.
  • 2022, 15 cohorts (Paluch et al.): the risk of death fell as daily steps rose, levelling off at about 6,000–8,000 steps for adults 60 and over and 8,000–10,000 for adults under 60.

Two honest caveats. These studies show associations, not proof that steps alone cause the benefit, since active people differ in other ways too. And the biggest gains come at the bottom: going from 2,000 to 5,000 steps matters far more than going from 9,000 to 12,000.

Steps by age

Based on the research above, reasonable daily targets look like this:

  • Adults under 60: aim for 7,000–10,000. The benefit keeps growing a little up to about 8,000–10,000.
  • Adults 60 and over: 6,000–8,000 captures most of the benefit.
  • Starting from a low base: if you're at 2,000–4,000 now, aim to add 1,000 a day each week or two. You'll get the biggest returns of anyone.

If you have a health condition or haven't been active for a while, check with your doctor before ramping up.

How to pick your own goal

  1. Find your current average. On iPhone, open the Health app, tap Steps and switch to W (week) or M (month).
  2. Set your first goal about 1,000–2,000 above that average.
  3. Once you hit it most days for two weeks, raise it again, until you're in the range for your age.

A goal you hit five days out of seven beats an ambitious one you give up on.

How to actually hit it

Knowing the number is easy. Walking it every day is the hard part. What helps:

  • Attach walks to things you already do: a walk after lunch, getting off the bus a stop early, walking calls instead of sitting ones.
  • Walk in short blocks. Ten minutes of walking is roughly 1,000 steps for most people, so three short walks add up fast.
  • Swap a scroll for a stroll. Most of us have an hour or more of phone time a day. Trading even 15 minutes of it for a walk covers a big chunk of the goal.

FAQ

Is 10,000 steps a day necessary?

No. Research links about 7,000 steps a day to most of the health benefit for adults. 10,000 still brings a little extra and is a good goal if you're already active, but it came from a 1965 pedometer advert, not a study.

Is 5,000 steps a day enough?

It's a big improvement over 2,000–3,000, and much of the benefit in the studies appears by 5,000–7,000. If you're at 5,000, adding another 2,000 is likely worth it.

How many steps is a mile or a kilometre?

Roughly 2,000 steps per mile, or about 1,300 per kilometre, depending on your height and stride. 7,000 steps is around 5 to 5.5 km.

How long does it take to walk 7,000 steps?

At a normal pace, about 60 to 70 minutes in total. Most people already take a few thousand steps in daily life, so the extra walking needed is usually 30 to 45 minutes.

Do steps from everyday activity count?

Yes. The studies counted all steps taken through the day, not just exercise. Your iPhone or Apple Watch counts them the same way.
