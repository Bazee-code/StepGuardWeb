How to limit screen time on iPhone, and make the limits stick

Updated 20 November 2026 · 6 min read

Short answer: open Settings › Screen Time, add an App Limit for the apps that eat your time, set Downtime for the evening, and turn on Lock Screen Time Settings so the limits need a passcode. Without a passcode, iPhone lets you tap "Ignore Limit" and carry on, which is why most people's limits stop working within a week.

1. Check where your time goes

Before setting limits, look at the numbers. Go to Settings › Screen Time › See All App & Website Activity. Switch between Day and Week and note:

  • the two or three apps with the most time,
  • how many pickups you have a day, and which app you open first,
  • which hours are worst (often late at night).

Those apps and hours are where limits will do the most good. You don't need to limit everything.

2. Set App Limits

App Limits give an app or a whole category a daily time budget.

  1. Open Settings › Screen Time › App Limits and turn it on.
  2. Tap Add Limit.
  3. Pick a category like Social, or expand it and choose specific apps such as TikTok or Instagram.
  4. Tap Next, set a time (for example 30 minutes), and tap Add.

Start a little below your current average rather than slashing it. A limit you can live with is one you'll keep.

3. Schedule Downtime

Downtime blocks everything except calls and the apps you allow, during set hours. It's the best tool for late-night scrolling.

  1. Go to Settings › Screen Time › Downtime.
  2. Turn on Scheduled and set the hours, for example 10:30 pm to 7:00 am.
  3. Under Screen Time › Always Allowed, keep only what you truly need at night, like Phone, Messages and Maps.

4. Lock it with a passcode

This is the step most people skip, and it's the one that matters. In Settings › Screen Time, tap Lock Screen Time Settings and create a passcode that's different from your phone's. With a passcode set, you can't change your limits without it, and you can turn on Block at End of Limit so a limit actually blocks the app instead of just showing a reminder.

The catch: if you set the passcode, you know it. If you're serious, ask a partner or friend to type in a passcode you don't know.

Why Screen Time limits fail

Screen Time is a good tool with a design weakness: it's built to remind you, not to stop you.

  • "Ignore Limit" is one tap away. Without a passcode, the limit screen offers one more minute, a 15-minute reminder, or ignoring the limit for the rest of the day.
  • You know your own passcode. When the urge hits, typing four digits is easy.
  • Limits ask nothing of you. Hitting a wall feels like a punishment, so it's tempting to remove it. There's no way to earn the time back.

Limits work best when getting past them takes real effort, ideally effort that's good for you.

Add friction you can't skip

FAQ

How do I set a time limit on an app on iPhone?

Go to Settings › Screen Time › App Limits › Add Limit, choose the app or category, set the daily time and tap Add. To make it block the app rather than remind you, set a Screen Time passcode and turn on Block at End of Limit.

Why can I still open apps after my Screen Time limit?

Without a Screen Time passcode, iPhone lets you ignore a limit for a minute, 15 minutes or the rest of the day. Set a passcode under Settings › Screen Time › Lock Screen Time Settings to stop that.

What's a good daily screen time limit?

There's no single right number. A practical start is to cut your current daily average for social apps by about a quarter, then lower it again once that feels normal. Focus on your worst apps and hours rather than total screen time.

Can I limit screen time without a passcode?

Yes, but limits without a passcode are reminders, not blocks. If you don't want a passcode, use friction instead: move apps off your home screen, turn off their notifications, or use an app like StepGuard that unlocks apps only after you've walked.
